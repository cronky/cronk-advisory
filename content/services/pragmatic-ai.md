engagements:
  - title: "AI readiness"
    body: "Where AI can genuinely help today, and where it cannot yet. We look at the processes, data, skills and governance an AI deployment would depend on, and find the [judgement line](/articles/the-ai-efficiency-fallacy/#where-the-line-falls): the points in each process where a named human must own the output.\n\nReadiness is as much about people as technology. Skills decay when people stop doing the work they are asked to check, so we look at that too."
  - title: "Stress-testing AI investment"
    body: "An independent challenge to the business case before you commit. Is the efficiency claim tested or assumed, and what is it being compared against? Does it budget for verification, skills and process redesign?\n\nWe also model the scenario most business cases skip: the one where AI volume means you need [more people, not fewer](/articles/the-pendulum-swings-back/), at the points only people can handle."
  - title: "Agentic AI governance"
    body: "Governance that works at the speed agents are built: guardrails embedded where creation happens, a gateway for policy, authorisation and audit, entitlements applied when information is retrieved, and escalation paths that give people the means to exercise their accountability.\n\nOur [reference architecture for consequential AI](/articles/reference-architecture-consequential-ai/) sets out the pattern."
  - title: "Right-sized AI"
    body: "The right model for the job, or no model at all. Deterministic software where it already does the job, smaller and local models where they are good enough, and cost and energy metered alongside latency, so that AI absorbs complexity rather than generating more of it."

We are firmly pro-technology, and AI can be genuinely useful. Pragmatic AI means being honest about what it can and cannot do, putting it where it absorbs complexity rather than adding to it, and designing for the people who remain accountable for what it produces.
